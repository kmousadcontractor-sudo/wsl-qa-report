Key findings

1. Inquiry page returns 502 under load (still failing)

809 of 2,889 inquiry-page requests (28%) failed with HTTP 502 Bad Gateway on 10 Nov, the same rate as on 9 Nov (761 of 2,756, 28%). The inquiry list and events stayed healthy at the same time, so the inquiry page (GET /support/requests/{ref}) is the weak point. 100 sign-ins at the same moment all succeeded, but took 6. See Load test.

2. 50-file limit fixed (10 Nov)

The 51st file now gets 409 "This inquiry has reached its limit of 50 files. To send more, start a new inquiry." instead of 500, and Send stays disabled so the reply cannot go out without the file. SUP-1010 accepts uploads again after the daily cleanup. See Fixed since 9 Nov.

3. Reopen works as specified (new)

The holder reopens and keeps the inquiry; another Tour Manager confirms "Reopen this inquiry and take it from …?" and the inquiry moves to them. An athlete reply on a resolved inquiry reopens it for the same Tour Manager. One wording note (RO-06). See Reopen.

Confirmed working

All upload rules (types, 10 MB, 3 per message, names, safety check, scan before opening, assignment, 5-minute window, 30 per hour). Take over works on open inquiries; resolved inquiries cannot be taken over (AC-07, AC-07b) and are reopened instead (RO-01, RO-02). Anti-spam held under load.

How Athlete Care works today

Athlete

  • Support → Athlete Care lists their inquiries with status ("Waiting for your reply").
  • Create Inquiry: Topic (7), Athlete, Subject, Message (10+ characters). No file upload on this form.
  • Replies can carry up to 3 files (JPEG, PNG, HEIC, PDF, 10 MB each).

Tour Manager

  • Athlete Care inbox: filters by status, topic and assignee; sorted by latest activity.
  • A new inquiry is Unassigned; replying needs Assign to me. Another Tour Manager sees Take over.
  • A Resolved inquiry shows Reopen: the holder keeps it; another Tour Manager confirms and it moves to them. Replying is locked until it is reopened.
  • New inquiry starts a conversation with an athlete.

Beach Marshal

  • Checks the athlete's account and earlier inquiries first.
  • Proposes an "Open a support ticket" card (topic, title, message, athlete) and waits for the athlete to confirm or cancel.
  • The inquiry arrives "via Beach Marshal" with a staff-only summary of what it checked.
  • Anti-spam: a "not a robot" check before the inquiry opens, and a daily limit on new staff inquiries to one athlete.

Load test: 100 athletes at the same time

10 Nov 2026 20:42:25 UTC · 100 virtual athletes on 8 QA accounts, all from one machine. All sign in at once, all try to open an inquiry at the same instant, then browse for about 60 s (inquiry list, their inquiry, events) with 0.2–0.6 s between requests. 8,867 requests in total, about 140 requests per second while browsing.

Inquiry page returns 502 under load (still failing)

809 of 2,889 inquiry-page requests (28%) failed with HTTP 502 Bad Gateway on 10 Nov, the same rate as on 9 Nov (761 of 2,756, 28%). The inquiry list and events stayed healthy at the same time, so the inquiry page (GET /support/requests/{ref}) is the weak point.

Sign-in slows down sharply

100 sign-ins at the same moment all succeeded, but took 6.2 s at the median and up to 14.0 s (9 Nov: 7.5 s and 17 s). Every other request answers in about 0.2–0.3 s when Dev is quiet.

Anti-spam held

Of 100 inquiries opened at the same instant, 5 were created ("QA LOAD …") and 95 were refused with 403 support.human_check_required ("not a robot" check). The inquiry page also has a per-person rate limit (429 bff.rate_limited).

Inquiry list and events stay fast

Inquiry list: median 248 ms, p95 551 ms. Events: median 221 ms, p95 252 ms, with no server errors, at about 140 requests per second.

Firewall blocked the test machine

After about 50 s of browsing (around 8,000 requests from one IP) every request got 403 Forbidden from the AWS load balancer (awselb), on all endpoints, for every account. This is an IP rate rule. Worth confirming the threshold: many athletes behind one shared network (a venue or club Wi-Fi) could be blocked together.

What this test can and cannot show: All 100 virtual athletes ran from one computer and shared 8 QA accounts (about 12 per account). That makes the per-person limit (429) and the IP firewall block (403) kick in sooner than they would for 100 real athletes on their own phones. The 502 errors and the slow sign-in are not explained by that and point at the server. A fair repeat needs more QA accounts and several machines or networks, or a firewall allow-list for the test machine.
